# Blogger and gift-guide outreach

The bottleneck on search is backlinks, not content. Search Console shows the
purpose-built pages ranking correctly for real buying queries — 846 impressions
on /gifts/christmas alone — but at position ~36, which yields nothing. Links
are what moves that.

Christmas gift guides are commissioned in **September and October**. A post
published in December is written in October. This is time-sensitive in a way
nothing else on the marketing list is.

---

## Who to approach

Search for these and collect the ones with a real audience and a contact page:

    personalised gifts for kids gift guide
    unique gifts for grandchildren blog
    christmas gift guide toddlers 2026
    best personalised baby gifts blog
    mum blogger christmas gift guide

Prioritise: parenting blogs, grandparent blogs, "gift guide" sites, and small
family lifestyle newsletters. A blog with 2,000 engaged readers and a real
link is worth more than a big site that nofollows everything.

Skip: anything asking for payment up front for a "sponsored placement" — that
is an ad, usually nofollowed, and not worth it at this stage.

**Volume matters.** Expect roughly a 10-20% reply rate and fewer conversions.
Twenty well-chosen emails is a realistic first push; five is not.

## Rules that decide whether this works

1. **Personalise the first line properly.** Reference something specific they
   actually wrote. A generic mail-merge is obvious and gets deleted.
2. **No attachments on the first email.** It looks like spam and hurts
   deliverability. Link instead.
3. **Send from hello@storykinbooks.com**, not a gmail address. The domain is
   verified in Resend, so it will deliver.
4. **One follow-up maximum.** Then stop. Being remembered as the person who
   pestered is worse than being forgotten.
5. **Deliver fast if they say yes.** Make the book the same day. A blogger who
   waits a week writes about something else.
6. **Never ask for a link explicitly.** Ask for a read. Bloggers who like the
   product link on their own; ones asked outright often nofollow out of
   irritation.

## Tracking

Keep a simple list: blog, contact, date sent, date followed up, outcome. Twenty
emails is enough to learn whether the pitch works. If nobody replies at all,
the problem is the subject line or the targets, not the product.
